Workstream A of the cold-start/readiness release blocker (spike 002-SP). Health probes currently reset the daemon idle TTL (src/daemon/ipc_server.rs accept_loop, ttl.reset() at the T049 comment) while the shim recovery monitor (src/shim/mod.rs) probes a non-ready daemon up to once per second. A never-ready daemon therefore never reaches its 4h idle shutdown. Live proof: PID 30528 ran ~2.5h, burned 72 CPU-minutes at ~198% of one core, held 1.84GB, never ready, never self-terminated. That process was CONFIRMED STILL RUNNING during the Revision 2 Stage pass. There is also no maximum-startup watchdog. INDEPENDENT of the cold-start root cause; caps blast radius regardless of why startup is slow.

SUPERSEDES A PRIOR COMPOUND LEARNING - EXPLICITLY AND NARROWLY. docs/compound/test-probe-resets-idle-ttl-livelock-2026-09-04.md (shipment 134-S) diagnosed this same mechanism but concluded that accept_loop should not distinguish meaningful requests in production, and fixed it TEST-SIDE ONLY. That scoping was correct on the evidence then available: the only observed victim was a TEST probe polling at 10Hz against a READY daemon. The new evidence invalidates that premise on three counts. (1) THE PROBER IS PRODUCTION: src/shim/mod.rs ships to users, so a test-side-only fix leaves the production livelock fully intact. (2) THE DAEMON IS NON-READY, SO THE RESET IS NEVER SEMANTICALLY JUSTIFIED. (3) THE FAILURE IS UNBOUNDED RESOURCE BURN, NOT A HUNG TEST. SCOPE OF SUPERSESSION: the 134-S mechanism analysis STANDS and is reused; only its production-side recommendation is superseded, and only for connections carrying no useful request. Genuine work MUST still hold the daemon alive (143.002-T), so 134-S premature-shutdown concern is preserved as an explicit non-regression requirement rather than discarded.
