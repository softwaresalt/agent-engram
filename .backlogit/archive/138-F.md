Independently owned reliability follow-up carved out of 137-F after PR #364 merged. Copilot review on PR #364 found that shim::lifecycle::check_health collapses every fetch_health error - including a terminal IpcError::VersionMismatch or a malformed/protocol-incompatible _health payload arriving after the initial readiness deadline - into a bare bool false. Both the request-triggered single-flight probe (src/shim/transport.rs forwarding_endpoint) and the bounded late-readiness monitor (src/shim/mod.rs spawn_late_readiness_monitor) therefore treat a permanently incompatible daemon as transient NotReady, emitting recoverable:true plus retry_after_ms forever instead of transitioning the session to a permanent fail-closed Degraded outcome. PLAN REVISION 2: Copilot review on PR #365 surfaced 12+ feasibility and correctness defects in the revision-1 plan AFTER the review-fix circuit breaker was exceeded, and Ship converted them to a Stage backlog handoff with an explicit claim prohibition. Revision 2 is a re-grounded redesign, not an addendum; every design claim is cited to file:line re-read at worktree HEAD. It additionally fixes a FAIL-OPEN RACE that revision 1 introduced and did not notice: the monitor holds its own watch sender, loops independently and sends Ready unconditionally, so it could overwrite a request-path Degraded and un-latch a proven-incompatible session. Scope: give the health probe a result-preserving return type carrying a CLOSED TerminalKind enum so terminal outcomes are separated from transient not-ready/unreachable outcomes and no daemon-supplied text reaches the wire or disk; make Degraded absorbing and monotonic across both publishers via watch::Sender::send_if_modified; route only transient outcomes through the recoverable cooldown path; add a late-terminal durable record with a write path that actually exists; and add deadlock-free, sleep-free concurrency coverage on a real clock seam that proves the session-wide single-flight mutex and 250ms cooldown actually suppress a second in-flight probe. Dominant risk preserved throughout: over-terminalization is worse than under-classification, so only protocol/content incompatibility proven by a received response becomes terminal while transport readiness stays retryable. Explicitly out of scope for 137-F/130-S, which were verification-only with no production-logic edits.
